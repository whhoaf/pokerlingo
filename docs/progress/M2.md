# Milestone 2 progress: Area C (reading the board) and D1

Status: Area C (C1 to C6) and D1 are done. `npm test`, `npm run typecheck` and `npm run build` pass. Nothing was pushed and `npm run deploy` was not run.

## What was built

**Engine**

- `src/engine/nuts.ts`: the nuts finder. `allHoldings` lists every two-card holding left on a board of 3 to 5 cards (1,176, 1,128 or 1,081). `strengthTiers` solves each one with pokersolver, sorts them and groups exact ties, so the first tier is the nuts. `boardThreats` enumerates every holding to find which can make a flush or a straight. `quickThreats` gives the same answer from the board alone, for cheap rejection in generators, and a test checks the two agree on 300 random boards.
- `src/engine/handEval.ts`: added `solve`, `compareSolved` and `describeSolved` so many holdings can be compared without solving twice.

**Skills** (`src/skills/C`, `src/skills/D`): generators, graders, explainers, worked examples and further reading (PokerRift trainer and Upswing hand rankings for C). Shared code is in `src/skills/C/common.ts` (hand wording, the showdown grader, a rank-by-rank explanation of why a hand wins).

- **C1:** your two cards plus five board cards. Four hand-name choices: the right one plus the three nearest categories, in rank order. Rare hands (four of a kind, straight flush) are built on purpose so they appear.
- **C2:** who wins. Wins and losses always come from different hand categories. About one question in nine is a split.
- **C3:** same hand category forced. 25% splits (many where the board plays); the rest are decided by kicker, pair rank or top card. The reason line says which.
- **C4:** the nuts. Four two-card holdings shown as mini cards. The answer is the strongest tier. The three distractors come from the next three distinct tiers.
- **C5:** Flush possible, Straight possible, Both, Neither. Answer from `boardThreats`. Boards have no pair (stated in the explainer).
- **C6:** counterfeiting. You make two pair with both hole cards on the turn. The opponent holds the other card of your high rank and an outside kicker card. The river pairs a board rank. If that rank is above your low pair, your low pair stops playing and the kicker decides (opponent wins, or a split when the kicker is on the board). If it is below, you still win. The grader is a pokersolver showdown, and feedback shows your final five cards.
- **D1:** pot 20 to 600 (multiples of 5), fraction 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 3/4 or the whole pot. Right within 5 chips or 5%, whichever is larger. No further reading yet (PRD: source to be found with the sizing rules).

**App**

- `ChoiceInput` renders choices that carry cards as mini cards (the text label stays for screen readers).
- `Feedback` takes an optional `highlightLabel` (new field on `GradeResult`), so pictures can say "Your best five", "The nuts", "Your final five cards" and so on. The default is still "Your outs".
- Skill map: Area C is live. Area D shows D1, then "Coming in a later update: D2 to D5." Area A still says "Coming in a later update."
- Home welcome text now says "count outs, price a call and read the board".

## Test counts

301 tests in 23 files, all passing (261 at the end of Milestone 1). New: nuts 15, handEval 2, C1 to C6 13, D1 4, registry and intro order 6.

Each skill runs 500 seeds. Every test checks the basics (no duplicate cards, same question when rebuilt from the seed, no dashes in any string, wrong answers grade as wrong), plus independent checks that use pokersolver directly and not the engine's wrappers:

- **C1:** the right label from `Hand.solve`; the nearest-category rule for distractors; every category appears at least 8 times; the highlighted five cards match pokersolver's.
- **C2, C3:** outcome from `Hand.winners`; categories differ (C2) or match (C3); the hand-written rank comparison behind the reason line agrees with pokersolver on every seed; mix of outcomes.
- **C4:** the nuts is the `Hand.winners` of all holdings; exactly one option is a winner; the three distractors have exactly 1, 2 and 3 distinct strengths above them. Options are two cards each.
- **C5:** every holding evaluated by pokersolver category. All four answers appear more than 70 times.
- **C6:** two pair on the turn using both hole cards; the river pairs a board card; showdown by pokersolver; "counterfeited" means the low pair no longer plays in pokersolver's best five; a counterfeit never wins and a clean river never loses.
- **D1:** answer and tolerance edges (exact, plus or minus the tolerance, just outside it), all five fractions, pots near 20 and 600.

Worked examples in every explainer are checked against pokersolver or the engine.

## Defects found and fixed

- **Next button clipped on tall feedback (found in the screenshot run, C6).** The sticky Next button sat inside the feedback block, and a sticky element cannot leave its parent. When the feedback began near the bottom of the screen the button was pushed about 8 px below the fold. It is now a direct child of the screen. This was a latent Milestone 1 bug that only a tall question exposed.
- **`handEval` returned more than five cards for a six or seven card flush**, and wrote the ace of a wheel as `1`. Both are fixed with tests. The first would have shown six cards as "your best five".
- D1's feedback said "Within 14.3 chips". It now states the rule.

## Deviations and decisions

1. **Intro order changed** (B and C interleaved, D1 before B8), as asked. The plan only said "interleaves". The exact order is my choice and is documented above.
2. **Prerequisites for C5 and D1:** C5 needs C1 (the plan gave none). D1 has none, as asked.
3. **C5 boards are unpaired.** On a paired board the best hand a flush or straight holding makes can be a full house, which would stop pokersolver's category from showing a flush. Unpaired boards keep the independent test exact. The explainer says so.
4. **C2 is mostly clear wins and losses with some splits; C3 forces the same category.** The plan said C3 "forces the same hand category", so C2 never uses the same category for a win or loss.
5. **C4 distractors** follow the plan (next strongest distinct holdings). On flush boards this means all four options can share the ace of a suit and differ in the second card. That is faithful to the plan and hard on purpose. Several holdings can tie for the nuts (for example three pairs of kings); the question shows only one of them.
6. **C6 design.** The plan's wording is loose ("you make two pair with both hole cards; a later board card pairs the board and your hand loses value"). A single pair on the board only hurts you against an opponent who shares your high card, so the opponent holds your high rank plus a kicker. Splits and "river changes nothing" cases are included so the answer is not always "Opponent wins". If you want a different counterfeit shape, the generator is `dealCounterfeit` in `src/skills/C/C6.ts`.
7. **`GradeResult.highlightLabel`** is a new optional field, added to the contract in `docs/PLAN.md`'s shape. The plan file itself was not edited.
8. **Area D map text** is "Coming in a later update: D2 to D5." for the rest, and "Coming in a later update." for Area A.
9. **No PRD and plan conflicts** were found this milestone.

## Known gaps

- **C4 is the slowest skill to generate.** It solves about 1,100 hands per question (roughly 70 ms in Node). A phone may take a few times longer, which should still be under a quarter of a second. The C4 test takes about a minute for 500 seeds. If it feels slow on the iPhone in Milestone 5, the nuts tiers could be cached per board.
- Total test time is about 80 seconds, almost all of it the C4 and C5 500-seed tests.
- D1 has no further-reading link yet.
- Same gaps as Milestone 1 for real-iPhone testing, service worker updates, screen readers and answer-time display.
- The C explainers fit one phone screen only roughly. C6's scrolls (it has a worked example with five cards and a long working list).
